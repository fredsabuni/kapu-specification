01 · Executive Summary

What is Kapu?

Kapu is a universal, multi-tenant lending rail that lets any Tanzanian shareholder borrow against their DSE-listed securities — instantly, digitally, and from any participating financial institution they choose.

The problem today

Securities-backed loans in Tanzania are still largely manual. A shareholder who wants to borrow against their portfolio has to coordinate between a bank, a stockbroker, and the CDS — paper-by-paper, signature-by-signature. Turnaround time is days, not minutes. Each bank that wants to offer this product rebuilds the same plumbing from scratch.

What Kapu does

Kapu acts as the shared rail. Shareholders see their full portfolio, request a loan, and choose from competing offers across multiple banks — all from a mobile app or a WhatsApp chat. Banks plug into Kapu's APIs, configure their loan appetite (rates, tenors, LTV, haircuts), and let Kapu's eligibility engine, lien orchestration, and disbursement flow do the rest.

↳ The shift in one sentence

The original BRD describes one bank automating one product. Kapu generalizes that into a market utility: one onboarding, one CDS link, one credit profile — usable across many banks, many products, many shareholders.

Outcomes Kapu unlocks

⏱️

Sub-5-minute TAT

From CDS link to disbursement, eligible flows complete straight-through with no human in the loop.

🏦

Lender competition

Shareholders see real offers from configured banks side-by-side and pick the best rate and tenor.

🔒

Controlled risk

Real-time CDS lien marking, dynamic haircuts, CRB integration, and forced-liquidation rails per lender.

📈

Market depth

Unlocks dormant value sitting in DSE portfolios; turns shares into productive, liquid collateral.

🔌

Plug-and-play for banks

A bank joins Kapu, exposes its LMS endpoints, configures product rules, and is live — without rebuilding the rails.

💬

Reach via WhatsApp

Shareholders without a smartphone-grade app experience can still onboard, view portfolio, and borrow via chat.

02 · Product Vision

One rail. Every bank. Every shareholder.

Kapu's design principle is universality. Ownership data lives at CDS. Credit history lives at the CRB. Money lives at the banks. Kapu simply routes between them — fast, securely, and with the shareholder firmly in control.

PRINCIPLE_01

Shareholder-first

A single Kapu account is the shareholder's identity across every participating lender. No re-onboarding. No re-KYC per bank. Portfolio, loan history and repayment status live in one place.

PRINCIPLE_02

Multi-tenant by design

Every bank on Kapu is a tenant with its own product configuration: rates, tenors, eligible securities, LTV bands, haircut tables, exposure limits, and routing rules.

PRINCIPLE_03

API-native, not file-based

No CSVs, no overnight batches. Every interaction with DSE/CDS, brokers, CRBs, and partner banks is real-time, idempotent, and signed.

PRINCIPLE_04

Open marketplace

When a shareholder requests a loan, Kapu broadcasts the request to eligible lenders, collects offers, and presents them transparently. The shareholder picks.

PRINCIPLE_05

Channel-agnostic

Mobile app for the full experience. WhatsApp chatbot for low-friction reach. Optionally: USSD, SMS, agent-assisted. Same backend, same outcomes.

PRINCIPLE_06

Auditable end-to-end

Every state change — OTP, eligibility, offer, lien, disbursement, repayment — is event-sourced and tamper-evident, ready for both internal audit and regulatory inspection.

Who is Kapu for?

👤

Shareholders

Anyone holding DSE-listed securities through a CDS account who wants to unlock liquidity without selling their position.

🏦

Banks & non-bank lenders

Institutions wanting to offer a securities-backed product without building the full broker/CDS/CRB integration stack themselves.

📊

Brokers & CDS

Counterparties who benefit from a single, standardised request channel for portfolio queries and lien instructions.

03 · User Journey

From "I have shares" to "money in my account" — in five steps.

The shareholder experience is intentionally minimal. Kapu does the heavy lifting in the background; the user only ever sees what they need to decide on.

Onboarding & first-loan flow

1
Enter CDS account

Shareholder downloads the Kapu app (or messages the WhatsApp bot) and enters their CDS account number.

2
Verify with OTP

Kapu cross-checks the CDS account, finds the linked phone number, and dispatches an OTP. Shareholder enters it.

3
See your portfolio

Real-time pull of all holdings: shares, bonds, units. Live valuation, eligible-as-collateral flag per security.

4
Request a loan

Enter desired amount and tenor. Kapu calculates max eligible amount and broadcasts to participating banks.

5
Pick an offer, sign, get funds

Compare bank offers side-by-side. Tap to accept. Digital signature, lien marked at CDS, money lands.

The returning-user flow

Once a shareholder is on Kapu, every subsequent interaction is faster. The CDS account is already verified, the credit profile is already scored, and outstanding loans are visible at a glance.

A
Open Kapu

Biometric / PIN authentication. Dashboard loads with portfolio value, active loans, next repayment date.

B
View status

See loan balance, accrued interest, days to next instalment, lien-locked securities, free portfolio value.

C
Act

Repay (early or scheduled), top up, request a new loan against free shares, or initiate early loan closure.

D
Confirm

Each action is confirmed with a digital signature and reflected immediately across Kapu and the partner bank's LMS.

↳ Universality, in practice

A shareholder can have a loan with Bank A and a separate loan with Bank B at the same time, against different parts of their portfolio — and manage both from the same Kapu screen. Each lien is tracked independently at CDS; each repayment routes to the correct LMS.

05 · Platform Modules

The building blocks of Kapu.

Each module is independently deployable, owns a clean bounded context, and communicates over well-defined internal APIs and events. Together they make up the Kapu platform.

M01 · IDENTITY & ACCESS

Identity & Onboarding

Owns the shareholder's Kapu account: CDS account verification, OTP issuance, KYC capture, biometric/PIN setup, session and device management.

  • CDS account ↔ mobile number bind
  • OTP issuance & rate-limiting
  • KYC document capture & OCR
  • Re-authentication policies
M02 · PORTFOLIO

Portfolio & Valuation

Real-time view of the shareholder's holdings via DSE/CDS APIs, with continuous mark-to-market valuation and dynamic haircut application.

  • Holdings sync from CDS
  • Live price feed from DSE
  • Per-bank eligibility filtering
  • Free vs lien-locked tracking
M03 · ELIGIBILITY ENGINE

Eligibility & Risk Engine

Computes maximum eligible loan, margin requirements, allowable tenor and interest band. Per-bank rule sets, per-instrument LTV and haircut tables.

  • LTV & haircut tables per tenant
  • Whitelist of eligible securities
  • Volatility-adjusted dynamic haircuts
  • Tenor & margin calculations
M04 · MARKETPLACE

Loan Marketplace

When a shareholder requests a loan, this module fans out the request to eligible banks, collects offers, and presents them for selection.

  • Offer fan-out & aggregation
  • Side-by-side offer comparison
  • Offer expiry & refresh
  • Bank-level routing rules
M05 · ORIGINATION

Loan Origination

Once a shareholder accepts an offer, this module orchestrates credit checks, agreement generation, e-signature, lien marking, and disbursement instruction.

  • CRB check via integration layer
  • Auto loan-agreement generation
  • E-signature / digital execution
  • STP vs manual review routing
M06 · COLLATERAL

Collateral Lien Management

Coordinates lien placement and release with brokers and CDS. Maintains the authoritative ledger of which securities are locked, for whom, against which loan.

  • Lien instructions to broker/CDS
  • Multi-bank lien tracking
  • Lien release on full repayment
  • Audit trail of all encumbrances
M07 · LMS GATEWAY

Bank LMS Connector

Universal connector that lets each bank plug their core banking and loan management system into Kapu via standard APIs. The bank's "edge" of Kapu.

  • Standard Kapu LMS API contract
  • Per-bank product configuration
  • Disbursement instruction outbound
  • Repayment posting inbound
M08 · REPAYMENT

Repayment & Servicing

Handles scheduled instalments, early repayment, partial top-ups, accrued interest tracking, and forced-liquidation triggers in default scenarios.

  • Schedule generation per offer
  • Early-liquidation flow
  • Default detection & aging
  • Auto-liquidation trigger to broker
M09 · NOTIFICATIONS

Notifications & Comms

Multi-channel orchestration of all shareholder, bank, and operational notifications: push, SMS, email, WhatsApp, in-app banner.

  • Templated, multilingual content
  • Channel preference & fallback
  • Delivery tracking & retries
  • Quiet-hours & throttling
M10 · CHATBOT

WhatsApp Chatbot

A second front-end for Kapu, exposing the same capabilities as the mobile app through structured WhatsApp conversations and interactive messages.

  • Intent classification & flow nav
  • Interactive list & button messages
  • Stateful conversation context
  • Hand-off to human support
M11 · ADMIN & OPS

Admin & Operations Console

Internal back-office: tenant management, product configuration, manual review queues, dispute handling, reporting and dashboards.

  • Bank tenant onboarding & config
  • Manual loan review queue
  • Reconciliation dashboards
  • Audit log explorer
M12 · LEDGER & AUDIT

Event Ledger

Append-only event store capturing every state transition across the platform. The single source of truth for audit, reconciliation, and regulatory reporting.

  • Event-sourced state changes
  • Tamper-evident hash chain
  • Replay & projection support
  • Regulator-ready exports
06 · Process Flows

How the modules dance together.

Four flows show the full life-cycle: registration, loan origination, back-office documentation & submission, and repayment / closure.

Flow A · Registration & Onboarding

Flow B · Loan Origination & Disbursement

Flow C · Repayment, Early Liquidation & Default

Scheduled instalment

On the due date, Kapu prompts the shareholder, debits the linked account (or initiates a wallet/USSD payment), posts the repayment to the lender's LMS, and updates the schedule.

Early liquidation

User taps "Close loan" in Kapu. Outstanding balance + accrued interest is computed in real time, paid in one shot, lien is released at CDS, securities are unlocked.

Default & forced liquidation

After the bank's grace period, Kapu instructs the broker to liquidate pledged shares on the DSE. Proceeds settle the outstanding; surplus returns to the shareholder.

Flow D · Loan Documentation & Back-Office Submission

The back-office paper trail that must be completed — in sequence, by the correct party — before a loan application proceeds to disbursement. Two forms, two required stamps, one National ID upload, and a per-counter processing fee.

Stamp mechanism — two paths

Digital stamp (in-app): The authorised party — bank officer or broker — applies their registered digital seal directly within Kapu. The document is cryptographically locked the moment both required stamps are present.

Download → stamp → re-upload: The party downloads the PDF, applies a physical stamp and wet signature, then re-uploads the scanned file. Kapu accepts the upload, version-locks it, and attaches it to the application record. Both paths produce a full audit trail.

Counter fee — TSh 20,000 per counter

A counter is each custodian or stock exchange the borrower is using to access the loan. Supported counters include CRDB, NMB, TCL and other participating institutions. Each counter selected incurs a flat fee:

Total fee = TSh 20,000 × counters selected

The borrower selects their counters in-app, the total is calculated automatically, and payment is collected before the application is forwarded to the bank for disbursement.

Kapu sits at the centre. Data flows continuously around it.

Kapu is the universal hub. Shareholders connect from the edges (Mobile, WhatsApp). Banks plug in via the LMS API contract. DSE/CDS, brokers, CRBs and payment rails sit on the outer ring. Architectural layers

LAYER · CHANNELS

Edge / Channels

Mobile (iOS, Android), WhatsApp Business API, optionally USSD & SMS fall-back, web admin console, and a bank-facing partner portal.

LAYER · API GATEWAY

API Gateway & BFF

Edge auth, rate limiting, request shaping. Backend-for-frontend tailoring per channel. mTLS for partner traffic, OAuth2/PKCE for user channels.

LAYER · CORE SERVICES

Core platform services

Identity, Portfolio, Eligibility, Marketplace, Origination, Collateral, Repayment, Notifications, Ledger — each independently deployable.

LAYER · INTEGRATION

Integration / connectors

Outbound adapters for DSE/CDS, brokers, CRBs, payment rails, e-signature providers, KYC document scanners, and bank LMS endpoints.

LAYER · DATA

Data & event store

Event-sourced ledger, transactional stores per service, analytical warehouse, encrypted document store, and audit trail with hash-chain integrity.

LAYER · OBSERVABILITY

Security & observability

Centralised logging, distributed tracing, metrics. Secrets in HSM/KMS. SIEM, fraud signals, anomaly detection on transaction streams.

08 · API & Integration Layer

Standardised contracts. Pluggable adapters.

Every external system that Kapu talks to has a defined adapter. New banks, new brokers, new payment providers slot in by implementing the relevant Kapu contract — no core changes required.

Bank LMS API (inbound & outbound)

The contract every participating bank implements to plug into Kapu. Standardised JSON over HTTPS with mTLS, signed payloads (HS256/RS256), and idempotency keys on all writes.

↳ A note on tenancy & configuration

Each bank tenant configures: eligible securities whitelist, LTV ratios per asset class, haircut tables, min/max loan amounts, allowed tenors, interest formula, fee schedule, STP threshold, grace period before forced liquidation, and customer eligibility filters (e.g. minimum credit band). The eligibility engine reads these per request and computes per-bank offers in parallel.

12 · Security & Compliance

Trust is the product.

A market utility moves real money against real assets. Security, privacy, and regulatory alignment are not features — they are the foundation.

🔐

Identity & access

Multi-factor authentication for every shareholder. Device binding with revocation. Strong customer authentication for high-value actions. Role-based access control with least-privilege defaults for internal and partner-bank users.

🛡️

Data protection

Field-level encryption for PII. KMS/HSM-managed keys with rotation. Tokenised handles for sensitive identifiers shared with bank tenants. Data minimisation in transit between Kapu and external systems.

📜

Consent & transparency

Granular, scoped consent capture: portfolio sharing, CRB lookup, lien placement, marketing communications. All consents are revocable and timestamped in the event log.

🧾

Audit & reporting

Tamper-evident event ledger with hash-chained entries. Pre-built regulator exports. Automated reconciliation between Kapu, banks' LMS, CDS, and payment rails. Annual third-party audits.

⚖️

Regulatory alignment

Designed to align with Bank of Tanzania prudential and consumer-protection guidance, CMSA capital-markets rules around lien marking, and the Personal Data Protection Act for shareholder data handling.

🚨

Fraud & AML

Velocity rules, device fingerprinting, behavioural anomaly detection. AML screening on shareholder onboarding. Suspicious-transaction monitoring with case management for ops review.

13 · Phasing & Roadmap

From pilot bank to market utility.

Indicative phasing. Each phase delivers shareholder value end-to-end; later phases broaden the ecosystem.

Phase 0 · Foundations

Discovery, partner alignment, regulatory engagement

Confirm legal and regulatory framework for a multi-bank lending utility. Sign first 1–2 pilot bank tenants. Establish CDS, broker, and CRB integration agreements. Finalise security and data-protection posture.

Phase 1 · MVP

Single-bank live, mobile + WhatsApp, end-to-end flow

Mobile app and WhatsApp chatbot. CDS integration for portfolio + lien. CRB integration. One pilot bank's LMS connector. Eligibility engine, marketplace (single offer), origination, lien, disbursement, repayment, closure. Admin console (basic).

Phase 2 · Marketplace

Multi-bank competitive offers

Onboard 3–5 additional banks. Marketplace fan-out and aggregation. Per-bank product configuration tooling. Side-by-side offer comparison UX. Manual review queues for partner banks. Reconciliation dashboards.

Phase 3 · Scale & Depth

Productisation & resilience

Forced-liquidation pipeline hardening. Advanced fraud and AML modules. Tiered customer support. Partner-bank self-serve onboarding. Public sandbox for new banks. Enhanced analytics for product, risk, and ops teams.

Phase 4 · Adjacencies

Expanding the rail

Expand collateral types beyond DSE equities (T-bonds, corporate bonds, CIS units). Bond-coupon-aware repayment models. Cross-border tenancy. Open data products for participating institutions and regulators.

14 · Execution Plan & Engineering Tracks

How we actually build Kapu — with four engineers, in parallel.

The work is sliced into four tracks that run side-by-side from week one. Each track has a single owner, a clear deliverable, and a short list of external dependencies that must be negotiated up-front so engineering never blocks on paperwork.

TRACK A · ENGINEER 1

Foundations & Platform

Cloud, CI/CD, identity, secrets, observability, event ledger. The ground every other track stands on.

TRACK B · ENGINEER 2

Backend Core Services

Portfolio, eligibility engine, marketplace, origination, lien, repayment. The brain of Kapu.

TRACK C · ENGINEER 3

Channels (Mobile + WhatsApp)

The mobile app, the WhatsApp chatbot, the BFF. Everything the shareholder actually touches.

TRACK D · ENGINEER 4

Integrations & LMS Gateway

DSE/CDS, brokers, CRB, payment rails, bank LMS connectors. Where Kapu meets the outside world.

↳ The parallelization principle

Work starts in parallel from week one. Track A ships shared scaffolding (auth library, event bus, deployment pipeline) that Tracks B, C and D depend on. Tracks B and D mock each other's contracts with stubs until real integrations land. Track C ships against a fake-data backend until Track B's endpoints are real. Each pair has a daily 15-minute sync; the team meets weekly to unblock seams.

↳ One thing to internalise

External APIs are the critical path, not the code. Every week the business team delays partner conversations is a week that compounds against the M3 and M4 milestones. Engineering will build against stubs until real sandboxes arrive — but a stub is not a contract, and surprises in the real APIs are where weeks disappear.

How we stay shipping with four engineers

🤝

Contract-first across tracks

Every cross-track interface is defined as an OpenAPI / event schema in week 1. Tracks consume each other's stubs immediately and replace them with real implementations as they land.

🧪

Sandbox-first integrations

Track D builds against the partner's sandbox the moment one is available. No track waits for production access.

🔁

Daily 15-minute pair syncs

B↔D and B↔C have standing 15-minute syncs. Decisions about contract changes are made in those rooms, captured the same day.

📦

Weekly demo, no exceptions

Every Friday the team demos a working slice end-to-end. Stubs are visible as stubs. Progress is "what a stakeholder can see and click", not "what's in the repo".

🪜

Milestone-gated decisions

Scope changes are deferred to the next milestone planning session. Mid-milestone, the team protects the slice it committed to.

🧯

One swing buffer

Whoever finishes their week's slice early is the unblocker for the others — usually plugging integration seams. There is no idle engineer; there is a swing role.

16 · Assumptions

What this specification depends on being true.

Regulatory

A market-utility model for securities-backed lending is permissible under Bank of Tanzania and CMSA regulations, with appropriate licensing and oversight. Digital signatures and lien marking via API are accepted as legally equivalent to paper-based processes.

Stakeholder integration

DSE, CDS, brokers, and the CRB are willing and able to expose stable, real-time APIs covering portfolio retrieval, pricing, lien marking, lien release, liquidation instructions, and credit reporting.

Bank participation

Participating banks have, or will build, the LMS endpoints required to plug into Kapu's contract, and will operate a sandbox environment for integration testing.

Customer reach

Shareholders have access to either a smartphone (for the mobile app) or WhatsApp (for the chatbot) and a phone number registered against their CDS account.

Risk parameters

Each bank tenant will define and own its eligible-securities whitelist, LTV bands, haircut tables, and exposure limits, with the ability to update them through Kapu's admin console.

Payment infrastructure

Disbursement and repayment can be reliably executed across the major Tanzanian payment rails (TIPS, mobile money, card, direct bank transfer) with confirmable, idempotent transaction semantics.

↳ Out of scope (for this specification)

Cost & benefit modelling, detailed financial projections, headcount plans, vendor selection, and detailed UI/UX wireframes are deliberately deferred to subsequent documents. This specification establishes the product, the modules, the architecture, and the contracts.
